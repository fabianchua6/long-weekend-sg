Use case: ui-mockup
Asset type: high-fidelity desktop website composition concept, landscape 1536×1024
Primary request: Create composition option A for “Long Weekend SG”, a one-page interactive Singapore public-holiday leave planner. This is a designed web app screen, not a poster and not a photograph.
Structure: no conventional navigation bar. A compact airport-style masthead across the top with the exact product name “LONG WEEKEND SG”, year tabs “2027 OFFICIAL” and “2028 FORECAST”, and two small toggles. Below it, a dense four-column by three-row grid of twelve individual month calendar cards fills most of the viewport. February is the focal card and is slightly lifted; its public holiday and suggested leave days form one continuous highlighted strip. A floating ticket-shaped recommendation panel sits directly above February with the exact large message “TAKE 2 DAYS → REST 5 DAYS” and two tiny alternative rows.
Content: show real-looking month grids, Monday-first weekdays, public-holiday markers, red leave-day stamps, a small legend, and source status. Use visible months JAN through DEC. The visitor’s job—scanning a whole year and discovering leave combinations—must be obvious from the screen.
Visual world: Changi-inspired planning desk without any Changi logo or trademark. Deep midnight airport navy ground, warm ivory paper month cards, Singapore red accents, brushed aluminium dividers, condensed departure-board numerals, tiny operational labels, tactile paper grain and subtle perforated ticket edges. Crisp, premium, highly legible, disciplined.
Signature interaction shown as a still: continuous break days illuminate like runway guidance lights across February; the ticket popup is the only dominant focal moment.
Constraints: desktop web UI only; no people, planes, skyline, travel photography, fake testimonials, gradients, glassmorphism, bento-dashboard chrome, generic SaaS cards, logos, or watermarks. Keep all core controls and calendar content semantic-looking and buildable in HTML/CSS. Preserve generous outer margins but make the calendar grid information-dense.
